# Skills

Characters in Cepheus Deluxe use a variety of skills to confront the challenges awaiting them among the stars, from piloting a starship to shooting a laser. Skills are a character’s greatest asset. Skilled characters are competent. 

* **Skill 1:** Represents someone who is employable.
* **Skill 3:** Denotes a trusted professional.
* **Skill 5:** Represents a world-class expert.

For example, a newly trained paramedic has Medicine 1; an experienced physician has Medicine 3; and a cutting-edge cyber-surgeon, skilled beyond the typical physician, has Medicine 5. The task resolution system takes this into account—professionals should automatically succeed at routine tasks (difficulty 6+) and easily perform average tasks (difficulty 8+), while experts will automatically succeed in routine and average tasks and easily manage difficult (10+) ones.

---

### A Note about the Cepheus Engine Core and System Reference Document

Cepheus Deluxe skills are somewhat different than those found in the Cepheus Engine Core rules but are easily compatible. Most notably:
* **Repair** is the combination of Electronics and Mechanics.
* **Admin** subsumes the Advocate skill.
* **Carousing** subsumes the Gambling skill.

### Zero-Level Skills

Normally, a character is either skilled in a task or unskilled. In the latter case, they suffer **DM-3** for lacking the appropriate skill. However, to facilitate play, we recommend that the Referee grant a few skills as "Zero-level" to each character at the start of the game. This means that while the character is not skilled enough to hold down a job with that skill, and does not enjoy a positive DM on rolls, the character is familiar enough with that skill to avoid the unskilled penalty (DM-3). 

The recommended Zero-level Skills for all adventurers are shown in the table below:

| Military and Paramilitary | Purely Civilian Characters |
| :--- | :--- |
| Athletics | Athletics |
| Driving | Drive |
| Gun Combat | Zero-G |

## Skill List

The skills included below are comprehensive, though there can be overlaps between two or more skills, depending on the context of the skill use in question, the players, and the Referee (we advise a certain amount of flexibility to keep the game moving).

#### **Admin:** Understanding and dealing with bureaucracies, businesses, and the law. Also covers forgery.
  * *Examples:* Throw Admin/EDU 10+ to deal with a complex government bureaucracy and get permits. Throw Admin/INT opposed by Investigate/INT to pass off forged documents as authentic. Throw Admin/INT or Admin/SOC 10+ to bluff your way past a routine customs inspection.
#### **Aircraft:** Controlling and using atmospheric aircraft. This skill covers navigation and basic maintenance, but not repairing damage.
  * *Examples:* Throw Aircraft/DEX 8+ to safely land an airplane in stormy weather without automatic guidance. Throw Aircraft/EDU 8+ to plot out a long flight path that maximizes fuel efficiency.
#### **Animals:** The care and training of animals. Also covers riding and general agriculture.
  * *Examples:* Throw Animals/DEX 8+ to avoid falling from a mount when the animal suffers a wound. Throw Animals/EDU 6+ to care for and feed farm animals.
#### **Athletics:** The ability to exert oneself physically. You may add the Athletics skill to appropriate physical characteristic rolls. This skill is never rolled unskilled.
  * *Examples:* Throw Athletics/END 6+ to swim against a powerful current. To move past obstacles while running, using Parkour-style moves, throw Athletics/DEX 8+. To climb a rope quickly, throw Athletics/STR 8+.
#### **Carousing:** The art of mingling in social settings to achieve your goals. Also covers gambling.
  * *Examples:* Throw Carousing/SOC 6+ to gather useful rumors at a party. Throw opposed Carousing/END to drink someone under the table.
#### **Computer:** Programming, working with computer hardware, and hacking. This skill also covers the use of sensors and communications gear.
  * *Examples:* Throw Computer/EDU 8+ to scan a derelict ship for signs of life. Throw Computer/EDU 12+ to hack a government server and download a database. Throw Opposed Computer/EDU to avoid detection and tracing while doing so.
#### **Deception:** Convincingly avoiding the truth and misleading other people. This skill covers sleight-of-hand and disguise as well.
  * *Examples:* Throw Deception/SOC 10+ to lie your way past a corporate sentry while posing as a visiting executive. Throw Deception/INT opposed by Investigation/INT to lie during a police interrogation and not be found out. Throw Deception/END 12+ to fool a polygraph lie detector test. Throw Deception/DEX 10+ to palm a small item (can be opposed by Recon or Investigation).
#### **Demolitions:** Working with explosives to blow stuff up! This skill also covers defusing said explosives before they blow up!
  * *Examples:* Throw Demolitions/INT 8+ to defuse a bomb of average complexity. Substitute DEX if the bomb is on a timer and is about to explode imminently. An Effect of -4 or worse will cause an accidental detonation. Throw Demolitions/EDU 10+ to demolish a bridge using the bare minimum of explosives. Throw Demolitions/INT 8+ to construct and plant homemade fragmentation mines on a defensive perimeter.
#### **Driving:** Controlling and using ground vehicles. This skill covers navigation, as well as basic maintenance, but not repairing damage.
  * *Examples:* Throw Driving/DEX 8+ to jump over a raised drawbridge with a motorcycle. Throw Driving/EDU 8+ to navigate through a desert on a long-distance rally race. Throw Driving/DEX 10+ to pull a bootlegger turn and parallel park, all in one smooth motion.
#### **Engineering:** Using and maintaining of starship drives, powerplants, and other systems specific to starships. Engineering is the required skill for Jump drive operation. This skill is not a substitute for the Repair skill.
  * *Examples:* Throw Engineering/EDU 8+ to estimate a ship’s performance and tonnage. Throw Engineering/INT 10+ to jury-rig a damaged power plant. More Engineering skill actions are presented in the Space Combat and Starship Operations chapters.
#### **Grav Vehicles:** Controlling and using gravitic vehicles. This skill also covers navigation and basic maintenance, but not repairing damage.
  * *Examples:* Throw Grav Vehicle/DEX 8+ to fly a grav-disc through a severe thunderstorm. Throw Grav Vehicle/DEX 10+ to fly at nape-of-earth altitudes at supersonic speeds.
#### **Gun Combat:** Using small arms, crossbows, and bows in combat.
  * *Examples:* Throw Gun Combat/DEX 8+ to hit a target in Effective range with a handgun. Throw Gun combat/DEX 10+ to hit a target at Maximum range. See the Combat chapter for more information.
#### **Gunnery:** Using vehicle-mounted or spaceship-mounted weapons.
  * *Examples:* Throw Gunnery/INT 8+ to hit a target in space combat. See the Combat and Space combat chapters for more information.
#### **Heavy Weapons:** Using plasma guns, rocket launchers, and other portable heavy weapons.
  * *Examples:* Throw Heavy Weapons/DEX 8+ to hit a target in Effective range with a plasma gun. Throw Heavy Weapons/INT 10+ to hit a target at Maximum range with a guided missile launcher.
#### **Investigation:** Keen observation, forensics, research, and detailed analysis.
  * *Examples:* Throw Investigation/INT 6+ to locate and extract fingerprints from a crime scene. Throw Investigation/EDU 10+ (or opposed by someone’s Admin/EDU) to uncover evidence of fraud or other irregularities in a business’ accounting practices. Throw Investigation/INT 10+ to find hidden levers, or to notice that a crime scene was disturbed before being discovered. Throw Investigation/INT 8+ to perform surveillance on a target.
#### **Jack of All Trades:** This special skill reduces the unskilled penalty (DM-3) by its level. For example, a character with Jack o’ Trades at level 2 would only suffer DM-1 to all unskilled rolls. You may not gain more than 3 levels in this skill and cannot improve it after character generation.
#### **Leadership:** Motivating and directing others, often in times of crisis.
  * *Examples:* Throw Leadership/INT 8+ to rally Shaken troops. See the Combat chapter for other uses of this skill.
#### **Liaison:** Negotiation and diplomacy in a myriad of social situations. This skill covers diplomatic meetings, haggling in interstellar trading negotiations, or at the souk, and talking down a gunman who is holding hostages.
  * *Examples:* Throw Liaison/SOC 8+ to strike a deal with a reluctant alien merchant. Throw Liaison/INT 8+ to read a room and negotiate with the person who really makes the decisions. Throw Liaison/EDU 10+ to understand proper etiquette when dealing with an honor-based culture. Throw Liaison/INT opposed by Liaison/INT to haggle over a price.
#### **Medicine:** Training in the medical sciences, from diagnosis and triage to surgery.
  * *Examples:* Throw Medicine/DEX 8+ to perform Trauma Surgery (see the Combat chapter). Throw Medicine/EDU 8+ to diagnose a common, but serious disease. Throw Medicine/INT 10+ to notice that someone in a small crowd is seriously ill.
#### **Melee Combat:** Fighting hand-to-hand, either with a weapon or unarmed.
  * *Examples:* Throw Melee Combat/STR 8+ to hit a target with a sword. The Combat chapter has more detailed examples of how to use the Melee Combat skill.
#### **Piloting:** Operating, controlling, and navigating interplanetary and interstellar space craft, both in real space and in jump space.
  * *Examples:* Throw Piloting/EDU 8+ to plot a jump course. Throw Pilot/DEX 10+ to land on a hostile planetary surface with no landing pad. See the Starship Operations and Space Combat chapters for more details on the Pilot skill.
#### **Recon:** Scouting for danger and spotting threats, including traps. Recon also covers performing forward observation for artillery.
  * *Examples:* Throw Recon/INT 8+ to correctly identify the composition of a concealed enemy force. Throw Recon/INT 10+ to identify a likely ambush kill zone. Throw Recon/INT 10+ to call down an airstrike on a large stationary target, such as a building. Throw Recon/INT 8+ to track a target in the wilderness. Throw Recon/INT 12+ to locate a devilishly hidden trap.
#### **Repair:** The ability to maintain, repair and build mechanical and electronic devices of all sorts. This skill also covers lockpicking, both mechanical and electronic. Ship-specific systems are handled by the Engineering skill, and Computers covers the use of electronic devices.
  * *Examples:* Throw Repair/INT 6+ to repair a damaged vehicle at a workshop. Throw Repair/END 8+ to do the same repairs, but in one night. Throw Repair/EDU 8+ to restore a vehicle that is no longer functioning. For all these tasks, the character will need an ample supply of parts.
#### **Science:** The multi-disciplinary scientific skill, as would be typical for a starship’s science officer.
  * *Examples:* Throw Science/EDU 8+ to identify rare minerals in an asteroid using the ship’s sensors. Throw Science/EDU 10+ to predict how a new compound might react with human physiology. Throw Science/EDU 12+ to understand the effects of a jump-space anomaly on the gravitational quantum tachyon sensor array.
#### **Stealth:** The ability to be unseen and unheard, both while stationary and moving.
  * *Examples:* Sneaking past a guard is an Opposed throw where the interloper throws Stealth/DEX while the guard throws Recon/INT, or Stealth/INT, whichever is better. Throw Stealth/DEX 8+ to hide from searchlights. Throw Stealth/END 8+ if the character has to hide and stay motionless for long periods. Bypassing more advanced sensors and security systems require specialized equipment and impose higher Target Numbers. Characters without Stealth or Recon roll to spot interlopers unskilled.
#### **Steward:** The care and serving of passengers and other guests. Steward skill adds to the throw for locating High Passage passengers (see the Starship Operations chapter).
  * *Examples:* Throw Steward/INT 6+ to impress an upper-class passenger and gain a valuable bit of information. Throw Steward/EDU 8+ to know some basic information about the cultural mores of a given passenger to make them more comfortable. Throw Steward/DEX 6+ to mend clothing and groom the crew to a presentable level. Throw Steward/INT 8+ to cook a sumptuous meal.
#### **Streetwise:** Familiarity with underworld society and the ways of working with it. The Streetwise skill adds to the throw for finding Low Passage passengers (see the Starship Operations chapter for more details).
  * *Examples:* Throw Streetwise/STR 8+ to intimidate gang members. Throw Streetwise/EDU 6+ to understand criminal slang. Throw Streetwise/INT 8+ to locate a fence or fixer. Throw Streetwise/INT 8+ to shadow someone in an urban environment. Throw Opposed Streetwise/INT to lose a shadow. Throw Streetwise/SOC 10+ to arrange a meet with important criminals.
#### **Survival:** Staying alive in the wilderness. This also covers “outdoor” skills such as tracking, foraging, navigation, and fishing.
  * *Examples:* Throw Survival/INT 8+ to track your quarry through wilderness. Throw Survival/EDU 10+ to navigate through a steaming jungle. Throw Survival/EDU 8+ to determine just what sort of apex predator is hunting you.
#### **Tactics:** Tactical planning and decision making, whether on the ground or in space.
  * *Examples:* Throw Tactics/INT to determine Initiative in combat (see the Combat chapter). Throw Tactics/EDU 12+ to predict the enemy’s battle plan using historical examples. Throw Tactics/INT 8+ to set up good firing positions for heavy weapons.
#### **Watercraft:** Controlling and using watercraft, including submarines. This skill includes navigating these vehicles, as well as performing basic maintenance, but not repairs.
  * *Examples:* Throw Watercraft/DEX 6+ to pilot a small motorboat through a storm without sinking. Throw Watercraft/DEX 12+ to sail a boat through a series of reefs and emerge unscathed, during a storm. Throw Watercraft/EDU 10+ to plot the circumnavigation of a planet.
#### **Zero-G:** Working and living under microgravity conditions. Also covers the use of space suits and heavy armor. When characters are in combat in microgravity, they should use the lower of their combat or Zero-G skill.
  * *Examples:* Throw Zero-G/DEX 6+ to traverse harsh terrain in microgravity conditions on an asteroid. Throw Zero-G/DEX 8+ to fire a weapon at boarders in microgravity. Throw Zero-G/STR 8+ to grapple someone in microgravity.

## Languages

Characters know a number of languages, in addition to their cultures' standard language, equal to their EDU modifier (see Characteristic Modifiers in Chapter 4: Character Generation). After character generation, characters may learn additional languages using the experience rules (see below) by purchasing the language for 8 XP. There are no skill levels of a language; either a character knows that language or they do not. The Referee is free to allow a Level 0 version of a language skill, representing a basic understanding of the language.

## Skill Advancement

Cepheus Deluxe characters can slowly advance in skill through experience. Players advance their characters by spending Experience Points (XP). A Character will typically gain 1 XP per session. The Referee might award 2 XP for a session where the player did an outstanding job, or if the character was very successful. 

Characters can only advance skills during downtime in game: during periods of relative quiet and inactivity. Note that advancement is incremental: for example, to increase a skill from Level 1 to Level 3, the character must first purchase Level 2 and only then Level 3. In general, use of XP should allow a character to increase one skill by one level per game year.

### Learning Languages

You may learn a new language by investing **8 XP**.

---

### Gaining Traits

You may gain a new Trait by investing **30 XP**. The character must meet all the prerequisites to purchase the Trait.

---

### Optional Rule: Improving Characteristics

Some Referees may allow characters in their games to improve their characteristics. If this is the case, then any characteristic may be increased via XP expenditure, except for Social Standing.

To improve a characteristic by one point, spend XP equal to **three times the new value**. So, to improve a characteristic from 7 to 8 costs 24 XP. 

The human maximum for any characteristic is 15, and Referees may wish to limit the number of times a given characteristic may be improved to 2 or 3. Social standing can only be improved by social advancement, which is accomplished through role-playing and successful adventuring.
